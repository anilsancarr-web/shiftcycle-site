# Privacy Policy — Shift Cycle

**Last updated: 10 November 2026**

## The short version

**Shift Cycle does not collect your data.** There is no account, no server
holding your roster, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. Everything you
enter stays on your device and — from version 1.2, while iCloud is on — in a
copy in your own iCloud that we cannot see, and in the Calendar app or a backup
file if you choose to put it there. All of it is described below.

---

## What we collect

**Nothing.**

We do not operate a backend. We do not collect personal information, usage
analytics, advertising identifiers, location data, contacts, or any other
information about you or your work. The two services the app does contact —
for app updates and for purchases — are described below, with exactly what
each one receives.

There is no sign-up, because there is nothing to sign up to.

## What stays on your device

The following is stored locally and is never transmitted to us:

| Data | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| Your rotation and its shift types | On your device |
| Individual day changes, notes and extra pay | On your device |
| Your pay settings (rates, premiums, overtime, tax), if you enter them | On your device |
| Your public-holiday country and the days you mark as holidays | On your device |
| Your language and week preferences | On your device |

If you use the widgets, this month's and next month's shifts are also written
to a shared container **on your own device** so the home screen and lock screen
widgets can draw them. They do not leave the phone.

From version 1.2, like other apps' data, this is part of your iPhone's own
iCloud or computer backup, under Apple's privacy policy, so restoring a new iPhone from that
backup brings it back. We never see that backup.

## The copy in your iCloud

From version 1.2, while iCloud is on for your iPhone, Shift Cycle keeps a copy of the data in the
table above — except the language and week preferences, which belong to each
phone — in your own iCloud, using Apple's iCloud key-value storage. It is
updated as you change things, so that on a new iPhone signed in to the same
Apple Account, or after reinstalling, the app can offer to bring your calendar
back. Apple stores it in your iCloud account under Apple's privacy policy; it
does not pass through us, and we cannot see or access it.

To stop it, turn off **Settings → Keep a copy in iCloud** in the app, on each
iPhone that has Shift Cycle: an iPhone with it on keeps saving a copy. Turning
it off deletes the copy that iPhone saved, and no new one is made. A copy from
another iPhone that is still waiting for you to bring it back or replace it is
left alone. Deleting the app does not delete the copy, so turn it off first if
you want it gone.

Deleting the app removes everything else on the phone. We cannot recover it,
because we never had it.

## Calendar, reminders and backups — only if you turn them on

- **Calendar.** If you tap "Add my shifts to Calendar", the app asks for
  calendar access and writes your shifts, with any day notes, into a separate
  calendar called "Shift Cycle" in the iOS Calendar app. It only reads, changes
  and deletes events in that calendar, and never touches your other calendars.
  If your calendars sync with iCloud, Apple syncs this one too, under Apple's
  privacy policy — it does not pass through us. That calendar stays in the
  Calendar app if you delete Shift Cycle; remove it from the app first, or
  delete it in the Calendar app.
- **Shift reminders.** Reminders are local notifications scheduled on your
  phone. No push server is involved and nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Backups.** "Back up my data" creates a file and hands it to the iOS share
  sheet. Where it goes — Files, iCloud Drive, AirDrop, email — is your choice.
  The app never uploads it.

## App updates

To receive bug fixes without a new App Store download, the app checks Expo's
update service (expo.dev) when it starts. That request carries the app
version, the platform (iOS), the release channel and a random identifier for
this installation. If the app failed to start after an update, the next
request also carries the technical error message from that failed start (at
most 1,024 characters), so that a broken update can be found and withdrawn. It
never carries your rotation, shifts, notes or pay rate.
Expo's privacy policy: [expo.dev/privacy](https://expo.dev/privacy)

## Purchases

Payments are handled entirely by Apple. We never see your name, your email
address, or your payment details.

To know whether you have paid, the app uses **RevenueCat**, which validates the
receipt Apple issues. RevenueCat receives an anonymous identifier and the
purchase receipt. It does not receive your roster, your shifts, your notes or
your pay settings — those never reach RevenueCat or us, and there is no
mechanism by which they could.

RevenueCat's own privacy policy: [revenuecat.com/privacy](https://www.revenuecat.com/privacy)

## Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any
age.

## Your rights

Data protection law gives you rights to access, correct, export and erase
personal data held about you. We hold none, so there is nothing to request —
your data is already entirely in your own hands, on your own device and in
your own iCloud.

## Changes

If this policy ever changes, the date at the top changes with it. Any change
that would involve collecting data — which is not planned — would be announced
in the app before it took effect, not buried here.
